## Introduction: Your House Warns You Before It Fails

Electrical installations don't fail suddenly — they give warnings. The problem is that most people ignore the signs until something burns, smells like smoke, or stops working completely.

In Venezuela, a large part of the housing stock is over 25 years old and was wired for much lower consumption than today. In the 80s, a house had a TV, fridge and iron. Today it has air conditioning, microwave, electric oven, multiple TVs, chargers and computers. The original wiring was simply not designed for this.

At ReformaT Venezuela we have inspected hundreds of homes in **Caracas, Valencia and San Diego**. These are the 7 signs that, when they appear together, indicate you need a rewire.

## 1. Breakers Trip Frequently

A breaker that trips occasionally can be normal (you turned on too many things at once). A breaker that trips **every week** is not normal: it means an overloaded circuit or a defective breaker.

The dangerous part: many people "fix" this by replacing the breaker with one of higher amperage. That is a serious mistake. The breaker is sized to protect the wire; if you put a bigger one, the wire overheats before the breaker trips. Result: fire risk inside the wall.

**The correct solution** is to redistribute loads and add circuits, not increase the amperage.

---

## 2. Hot Outlets or Burning Smell

Touch your outlets after using an appliance. If they are warm or hot, there is a loose connection or overloaded wire generating heat. Heat degrades the wire insulation and is the prelude to a short circuit.

A burning smell, even if slight, means something is already overheating. This doesn't fix itself: turn off the circuit and have it checked.

---

## 3. Flickering Lights

A light that flickers when you turn on the air conditioner or microwave indicates a voltage drop from overload or a poor connection in the panel. It's not just annoying: it's a symptom that the installation cannot handle the demand.

---

## 4. Aluminum or Very Old Wiring

If your home is pre-1990, it probably has aluminum wire or copper with cloth insulation. Aluminum wire oxidizes at connections, generates heat and is a known cause of residential fires.

The only real solution is to replace it with modern copper wire with thermoplastic insulation (THHN/THWN).

---

## 5. No Physical Ground

If your outlets only have two slots (no third ground pin), your installation has no grounding. This means a current leak has nowhere to escape, and whoever touches it becomes the path to ground.

In kitchens and bathrooms, where there is water, lack of ground is especially dangerous. A modern installation includes physical grounding with a rod and three-prong outlets throughout the house.

---

## 6. Total Dependence on Power Strips and Extensions

If you need power strips and extensions in every room because there aren't enough outlets, your installation is insufficient for your current life. Overloaded power strips are another frequent cause of fires.

The solution is not to buy more power strips: it is to add electrical points with proper circuits.

---

## 7. The Panel Looks Like a Museum

Open your electrical panel. If you see breakers from unknown brands, tangled wires, taped splices, or an old fuse box, it's time to modernize. A neat panel with thermomagnetic and differential protections is the heart of a safe installation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does a rewire require opening all the walls?
Not always. If there is accessible conduit pipe, it can be rewired through it. Walls are only opened when the cable is embedded directly in concrete or when new points need to be added.

### Is it dangerous to keep using the installation in the meantime?
It depends on the severity. If there is a burning smell, hot outlets or breakers tripping constantly, reduce the load and have it checked soon. Don't wait for something to fail.
