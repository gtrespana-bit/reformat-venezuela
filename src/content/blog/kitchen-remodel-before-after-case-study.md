## Starting Point: A 20-Year-Old Kitchen

The original kitchen showed the classic symptoms of two decades of use:

- **Inefficient layout:** limited work surface and poorly utilized storage
- **Outdated installations:** electricity without dedicated circuits and minor plumbing leaks
- **Worn materials:** stained countertops, misaligned cabinet doors, damaged flooring
- **Poor lighting:** single central light point, dark work zones
- **Outdated aesthetics:** colors and finishes from another era

The client’s goal: a modern, functional kitchen with good lighting and space for family cooking, without expanding the footprint.

---

## Phase 1: Design and Planning

Before touching a wall, everything was defined on paper:

- **New L-shaped layout** with central island to increase work surface
- **Optimized work triangle** (refrigerator → sink → stove)
- **Material selection:** quartz countertop, lacquered MDF cabinets, porcelain tile flooring
- **Lighting plan:** general lighting + under-cabinet lights in work areas
- **New installations:** dedicated electrical circuits and complete plumbing

This phase took two weeks and prevents most surprises later. To understand the cost of a kitchen like this, see our [kitchen remodel pricing guide](/en/blog/kitchen-remodel-cost-caracas-valencia-2026/).

---

## Phase 2: Demolition

With the design approved, demolition began:

- Removal of old cabinets, countertops, and appliances
- Lifting of existing flooring
- Chipping of wall coverings
- Disassembly of old installations

This stage lasted 4 days and is the noisiest. In an apartment, we coordinated with the condominium to respect permitted hours.

![Demolition of the old kitchen](/images/integrales-demolicion.webp)

---

## Phase 3: New Installations

With the kitchen in rough condition, we installed:

- **Electrical:** new dedicated circuit for oven and microwave, under-cabinet lighting points, additional outlets on the countertop
- **Plumbing:** new cold and hot water lines, relocated drain for the new sink position
- **Preparation** for the range hood with exterior vent

This stage is invisible in the end, but it ensures the kitchen works well for decades. For details on what’s included, see our [electrical installation guide](/en/blog/electrical-installation-prices-venezuela).

---

## Phase 4: Wall and Floor Coverings

We installed:

- **Porcelain tile** on the floor — stain and traffic resistant
- **Ceramic backsplash** on work walls
- **Surface leveling** before installation

Porcelain was chosen for durability and easy cleaning — two critical factors in a kitchen. If you’re deciding between materials, compare them in our [porcelain vs ceramic guide](/en/blog/porcelain-tile-vs-ceramic-tile-guide/).

---

## Phase 5: Cabinets and Countertops

The most visible part:

- **Cabinets** in lacquered MDF with soft-close hardware
- **Quartz countertop**, resistant to stains, heat, and scratches
- **Central island** with storage and breakfast space
- **Internal organization:** drawers with dividers, maximized corner space

Cabinet fabrication was done in parallel with previous stages to keep the total timeline short.

![Kitchen with central island and quartz countertop](/images/cocina-isla-central.webp)

## Phase 6: Finishes and Lighting

The final details that transform the space:

- **LED lighting** under upper cabinets (shadow-free work zone)
- **Pendant lights** over the island
- **Modern faucet** with pull-out spray
- **Painting** in non-tiled wall areas
- **Appliance and hardware installation**

## Final Result

The kitchen went from a dark, inefficient space to a modern, bright, and functional kitchen:

- **Work surface** practically doubled with the island
- **Storage** optimized with custom cabinets
- **Zoned lighting:** general, task, and ambient
- **New installations** with dedicated circuits and no leaks
- **Contemporary aesthetics** that add value to the home

![Final result of the remodeled kitchen](/images/cocina-encimera-cuarzo.webp)

The complete project took **5 weeks** from the start of demolition to handover — on schedule because all material decisions were made before work began.

---

## Lessons from This Project

What we learned (and applies to any kitchen):

1. **Define everything before demolition** avoids delays and expensive changes
2. **New installations are the invisible investment** that pays off the most
3. **Zoned lighting** changes perception more than expensive finishes
4. **Quartz countertop** is one of the best decisions for durability
5. **Coordinating cabinet fabrication in parallel** shortens the total timeline

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does a kitchen remodel like this cost?

It depends on size and materials. A medium kitchen with island and quartz usually falls in the mid-to-high range. See real ranges in our [kitchen pricing guide](/en/blog/kitchen-remodel-cost-caracas-valencia-2026/).

### Can I use the kitchen during the work?

Not during demolition and installations. We recommend setting up a temporary kitchen (microwave, portable fridge) in another area of the house.

### Is a central island worth it?

Yes, if the space allows. It adds work surface, storage, and a social point. In small kitchens, a peninsula may be better.

### What countertop material do you recommend?

Quartz offers the ideal balance between durability, aesthetics, and low maintenance. Natural granite is more heat-resistant but requires sealing.
