## What a Complete Electrical Installation Includes

Before talking about prices, you need to know what you're paying for. A professional residential electrical installation includes:

- **Main panel (breaker box)** with thermomagnetic and differential protections
- **Copper wiring** of adequate gauge (THHN/THWN) in separate circuits
- **Independent circuits** for kitchen, bathrooms, air conditioning and laundry
- **Light points and outlets** according to code (minimum one every 1.8 m of wall)
- **Physical grounding** with ground rod
- **Conduit pipe** PVC or EMT depending on the zone
- **Continuity and insulation tests and certification**

If a quote doesn't detail these points, it is not a serious quote.

## Factors That Raise or Lower the Price

### 1. Age of the home
A house from the 70s or 80s probably has aluminum wire or insufficient gauge, without physical ground. Replacing all of that costs more than installing from scratch in a new build, because walls have to be opened and then repaired.

### 2. Accessibility
If the wiring runs through embedded conduit in concrete, the work is slower and more expensive than if it runs through accessible conduit or raceway.

### 3. Material quality
A generic breaker costs a fraction of a recognized brand (Square D, Siemens, ABB). The difference: the generic may not trip when it should. On protections, you don't save.

### 4. Number of circuits
More circuits = more cable, more breakers, more labor. But also more safety and capacity. A modern kitchen with oven, microwave, dishwasher and refrigerator needs a minimum of two dedicated circuits.

## Signs You Need a Rewire

If your home has any of these, don't leave it for later:

- Breakers trip frequently
- Outlets are hot to the touch or smell burnt
- Lights flicker when you turn on an appliance
- You have aluminum or cloth wiring (homes before 1990)
- No physical ground in outlets (only two prongs)
- You use power strips and extensions for everything because there aren't enough points

## Why Prices Vary So Much Between Quotes

If you ask for three quotes and one says $1,500 and another $3,500 for "the same thing," it's not the same. Almost always the difference is in:

1. **Wire gauge** — using 12 gauge where 10 is needed is an overload risk
2. **Number of circuits** — grouping everything into few circuits is cheaper but overloads
3. **Grounding** — many omit it to lower the price
4. **Warranty** — work without warranty is cheaper until it fails

A suspiciously low quote usually means something from the list above is being omitted.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does a complete electrical installation take?
A 2-3 bedroom apartment takes between 5 and 10 business days, depending on whether walls need to be opened. A full house can take 2-3 weeks.

### Can I live in the house during the work?
In most cases yes, working by zones. There will be days without electricity in specific areas, but it is planned to minimize inconvenience.

### What warranty does professional electrical work have?
At ReformaT we guarantee the labor. Materials have the manufacturer's warranty. A well-done installation lasts 25-30 years without major intervention.

### Do I need condominium permission?
For internal changes to your apartment generally no, but if you touch the main panel or meter, yes. Consult with the condominium board before starting.
