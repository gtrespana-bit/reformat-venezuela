## Mistake #1: Not Having Your Own Contingency Fund

### The Reality of Construction Work

No remodel turns out exactly as drawn on paper. Behind the tiles, under the floor or inside the walls you can find:

- Old pipes that need replacement
- Electrical wiring that doesn't meet current code
- Hidden moisture or leaks
- Structural unevenness that requires additional preparation

**Industry statistic:** In 80-90% of remodels, technical surprises appear. The average is usually 10-20% of the base budget.

### How to Manage It Correctly

A transparent professional **does not charge you a "contingency fund" in advance**. What they should do is:

1. **Warn you** of the real possibility of surprises
2. **Recommend** that you reserve 10-15% separately from your total budget
3. **Communicate immediately** if something comes up, with a closed quote for that item before executing it

**Never sign with someone who tells you "there are no surprises here."** That is not realism, it is naivety or bad faith.

### Real Case
A couple budgeted $8,000 for their bathroom. When they opened the walls, they found an old fiber-cement waste stack that urgently needed replacement ($650). Because they had their own reserve fund, they approved it without stress. The project finished on time and without arguments.

**Lesson:** Surprises are not "contractor extras." They are technical realities. Having your own financial cushion gives you peace and decision-making power.

## Mistake #2: Comparing Quotes Only by the Final Price

### The Global Number Trap

You receive three quotes:
- Company A: $7,500
- Company B: $9,200
- Company C: $11,000

Which one do you choose? Most people look only at the number. **Big mistake.**

A professional quote is not a number. It is a **detailed document** that must include:

- Materials (brand, model, thickness, finish)
- Labor (estimated hours, specialty)
- Permits and municipal fees
- Debris management and removal
- Final cleaning
- Written warranty
- Execution timeline

### How to Compare Correctly

Create a simple table:

| Concept                    | Company A | Company B | Company C |
|----------------------------|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| Total price                | $7,500    | $9,200    | $11,000   |
| Breakdown by items         | ❌ No     | ✅ Yes    | ✅ Yes    |
| Material specifications    | Generic   | ✅ Detailed | ✅ Detailed |
| Permits included           | ❌ No     | ✅ Yes    | ✅ Yes    |
| Waste management           | ❌ No     | ✅ Yes    | ✅ Yes    |
| Warranty                   | ❌ Verbal | ✅ 1 year | ✅ 2 years |
| Written timeline           | ❌ No     | ✅ Yes    | ✅ Yes    |

**Conclusion:** Cheap that doesn't specify usually ends up expensive. Transparent that details protects you.

## Mistake #3: Forgetting Real Indirect Costs

### What You Must Count (And What You Shouldn't)

Many "cheap" quotes omit mandatory items to look better on paper. Then they appear as "extras."

**REAL indirect costs that should be included or clear from the start:**

| Concept                          | Why it's necessary          | Approximate Range |
|----------------------------------|-----------------------------|-------------------|
| Minor/major work permit          | Mandatory municipal         | $100-$400         |
| Electrical/plumbing certificate  | Safety and regulations      | $150-$300         |
| Debris container and removal     | Legal and practical         | $250-$500         |
| Professional final cleaning      | Ready-to-use delivery       | $150-$300         |
| Protection of non-remodeled areas| Avoid collateral damage     | $100-$200         |

**What should NOT appear in a remodel quote:**
- Client accommodation, meals or travel expenses
- "Equivalent materials" without specifying brand
- Global items without breakdown
- "Estimated" dates without penalty for delay

### How to Verify
Always ask: *"Does this quote include permits, debris removal, certificates and final cleaning?"* If the answer is vague or negative, be suspicious.

## Mistake #4: Paying in Advance Without Clear Milestones

### The Risk of Losing Control

Paying 50% or more before starting takes away your negotiation power and protection. If the work doesn't progress or the quality is not as agreed, they already got paid.

### Professional Payment Structure

A serious contract links payments to **verifiable milestones**, not dates:

1. **Signing and start:** 10-15%
2. **Demolition and debris removal:** 15%
3. **Installations (electrical/plumbing) certified:** 25%
4. **Cabinets and finishes installed:** 30%
5. **Final delivery, cleaning and inspection:** 15-20%

**Advantage:** You only pay when you see real and verifiable progress. If something doesn't comply, it gets fixed before the next payment.

## Mistake #5: Signing Without Reading the Contract

### Phrases That Should Alert You

❌ *"Prices subject to change without prior notice"* → Translation: They can raise the price whenever they want  
❌ *"Similar quality materials"* → Translation: They promised one thing, they'll put another  
❌ *"Estimated timeline"* → Translation: There is no real deadline  
❌ *"Warranty subject to evaluation"* → Translation: The warranty is not binding

### What Your Contract Should Have

✅ Closed and definitive price (except for changes you request in writing)  
✅ Technical specification of materials (brand, reference, finish)  
✅ Start date and delivery date with consequences for delay  
✅ Payment schedule linked to milestones  
✅ Minimum 1-2 year written warranty  
✅ Name and contact of the site supervisor  
✅ Resolution clause if deadlines or quality are not met

**Tip:** If they don't give you a written contract, don't sign. Trust is demonstrated with paper, not with words.

## Mistake #6: Choosing by Price, Not by Real References

### How to Verify for Real

"I don't know him, but he has a good price" is the phrase that generates the most regrets.

**Real steps to verify:**
1. Ask for 2-3 addresses of completed projects in the last 6 months
2. Call those clients. Ask: Did they meet deadlines? Did they respect the budget? How did they resolve problems?
3. Verify they have a business license and current liability insurance
4. Check real presence: office/workshop, professional website, verifiable reviews (not just generic 5 stars)

A serious company has no objections to you verifying their work. One that evades probably has something to hide.

## Mistake #7: Not Planning Work Logistics

### What Nobody Tells You Until It Starts

Remodeling is not just "tear down and put up." It is logistics:

- Where will you store your furniture and belongings during the work?
- How will you protect areas that are not being remodeled?
- Who will have the keys and how will access be managed?
- Will water/electricity be available during the work?

**Planning this prevents:**
- Damage to furniture from dust or humidity
- Delays due to lack of access or materials
- Misunderstandings with neighbors about hours or noise
- Unnecessary stress during execution

A good professional helps you organize this before starting. They don't improvise on the fly.

## Your Checklist Before Signing Any Quote

Print this and use it:

### ✅ Before Requesting Quotes
- [ ] Define exactly what you want to remodel (detailed list)
- [ ] Research real prices of materials you like
- [ ] Calculate your maximum budget (including 10-15% own contingency)
- [ ] Decide acceptable payment methods (never >20% upfront)

### ✅ When Receiving Quotes
- [ ] They are broken down by items, not a global number
- [ ] They specify brands, models and finishes
- [ ] They include permits, debris removal and final cleaning
- [ ] They have clear start and delivery dates
- [ ] They include written warranty
- [ ] They link payments to verifiable milestones

### ✅ Before Signing
- [ ] Verify company license and insurance
- [ ] Call at least 2 references from recent projects
- [ ] Read THE ENTIRE contract, especially price, timeline and warranty clauses
- [ ] Confirm who the supervisor will be and how to contact them
- [ ] Keep a signed copy from both parties
