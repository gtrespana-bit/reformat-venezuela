## Introduction: The Paperwork Nobody Explains to You

Remodeling an apartment in a condominium in Venezuela is not just about knocking down walls. In most buildings, you need written authorization from the board, and ignoring this step is one of the most expensive mistakes you can make.

At ReformaT Venezuela we have managed permits for hundreds of projects in **Caracas, Valencia, San Diego and Carabobo**. This is the practical 2026 guide so you don't get stopped mid-project.

---

## When Do You Need Authorization?

In 90%+ of premium and medium buildings, **any work** that involves:

- Demolition or structural changes
- Modification of installations (electrical, plumbing, gas)
- Change of floors or wall coverings in common areas
- Installation of air conditioning units on facade
- Moving of wet areas (kitchen or bathroom)

...requires prior written approval from the Condominium Board (Junta de Condominio).

Even "just painting" sometimes requires notification.

---

## Documents Usually Required

Typical requirements (varies by building):

1. Formal application letter describing the work
2. Plans or sketches of the intervention
3. Contractor information (RIF, insurance, experience)
4. Detailed timeline
5. Security deposit ($500 - $2,000)
6. Liability insurance policy ($200 - $500)
7. Neighbor notification (in some buildings)

**Pro tip:** A good company prepares all this documentation for you.

---

## Most Common Problems (and How to Avoid Them)

- Starting without authorization → work stopped + fines
- Not respecting work hours → neighbor complaints
- Not protecting common areas → extra charges or lawsuits
- Modifying facade without approval → restoration order
