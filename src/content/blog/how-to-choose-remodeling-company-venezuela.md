## Introduction: The Most Important Decision in Your Remodel

Choosing the wrong remodeling company costs more than choosing the right one. A low budget can end up costing twice as much when you have to redo the work, fix defects, or finish a half-abandoned project.

## 1. Real and Verifiable Experience

It’s not enough to say “I’ve been doing this for years.” Ask for:

- **Real project portfolio** with before-and-after photos
- **References from previous clients** you can contact
- **Years of track record** and stability (a company that disappears every year is a red flag)

A serious company has no problem showing you their work. If they make excuses for not showing projects, be suspicious.

---

## 2. Detailed Written Quote

This is the most effective filter. A professional quote must include:

- **Breakdown by line items** (demolition, masonry, electrical, plumbing, finishes)
- **Specified materials** (brand, model, quality)
- **Labor separated** from materials
- **Estimated timelines** per phase
- **Clear payment terms**
- **What is NOT included** (to avoid surprises)

If they give you a global “eyeball” number without breakdown, it’s not a quote — it’s a gamble. Always compare at least three detailed quotes. ## 3. Written Warranty

Ask directly: **what warranty do you offer and for how long?**

A reliable company guarantees its labor in writing (typically 1 year minimum) and respects the manufacturer’s material warranty. If the answer is “don’t worry, we’re here,” without documentation, there is no real warranty.

---

## 4. Clear Contract

For sizable remodels, there should be a contract that specifies:

- Exact scope of work
- Total price and payment schedule
- Start and delivery dates
- Responsibilities of each party
- How changes or unforeseen issues are handled
- Warranty and conflict resolution

A contract protects both parties. If the company resists signing one, it’s a red flag.

---

## 5. Own Team vs. Subcontracting

Ask who will actually do the work. There are two models:

- **Own team:** the company has its own professionals. More quality control and direct responsibility.
- **Subcontracting:** the company coordinates third parties. It can work, but it dilutes responsibility.

It’s not bad to subcontract specialists (for example, a marble worker), but you need to know who is responsible if something goes wrong.

---

## 6. Communication and Professionalism

Evaluate how they treat you from the first contact:

- Do they respond quickly and clearly?
- Do they visit the site before quoting? (quoting without visiting is a bad sign)
- Do they explain options without technical jargon?
- Do they keep appointments?

The way they treat you before hiring is a preview of how they will treat you during the project.

---

## 7. Licenses, Permits, and Insurance

Ask:

- Do they handle the **permits** for the work? (especially in condominiums)
- Do they have **liability insurance**?
- Do they comply with safety standards on site?

For apartment remodels, condominium permits are critical. In our article on [permits for remodeling in condominiums](/en/blog/remodel-permits-apartment-condominium) we explain what you need.

---

## 8. Reasonable Payment Terms

Be suspicious of anyone who asks for 100% upfront. A reasonable scheme is:

- **Deposit** (30-40%) to purchase materials
- **Milestone payments** as the work progresses
- **Final payment** (10-20%) upon delivery and verification

Never pay for work not yet performed. Payments must be tied to concrete progress.

---

## 9. Specialization

A company that “does everything” (remodels, gardening, pools, air conditioning, moving) is usually less reliable than one specialized in residential remodeling. Specialization indicates focus and accumulated experience.

---

## Red Flags: Run If You See This

- Suspiciously low price (well below everyone else)
- Verbal quote without breakdown
- Asks for all the money upfront
- No portfolio or references
- Pressures you to decide “right now”
- Refuses to sign a contract
- Starts work without defining the full scope

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many quotes should I request?
At least three, always detailed. Compare equivalent line items, not just the total.

### Is the cheapest quote the best?
Almost never. If one is much lower, it probably omits items, uses inferior materials, or doesn’t include a warranty. Cheap remodels usually end up expensive.

### How do I know if a company is legitimate?
Real portfolio, verifiable references, written quote, contract, warranty, and willingness to visit the site before quoting.
